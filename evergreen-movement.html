Roots

How a movement actually starts

Stage one

A classroom, then a hundred

Evergreen starts in schools — teaching kids to read a watershed, a soil sample, a bill moving through committee. Education is the entry point, not the whole story.

Stage two

Neighbors become organizers

Parents and teachers who show up for a lesson end up showing up at a hearing. We give them the language and the paperwork to turn concern into testimony.

Stage three

Policy catches up

Local ordinances on runoff, tree canopy, and emissions don't write themselves — they get written because enough people in the room understood the stakes.

Right now

You, building the ground floor

We're small on purpose, for now. Every volunteer who joins early shapes what this becomes — the curriculum, the tools, the tone of the whole movement.

Programs

Where the work actually happens

Flagship

Field Literacy

A K-12 curriculum teaching students to read their own local ecosystem before they're asked to save it — soil, water, air, and who's accountable for each.

01

Testify

Plain-language briefings that turn residents into confident public commenters at city council and planning meetings.

02

Canopy Watch

Volunteer-run tree canopy mapping that feeds directly into local zoning and development review.

03

Policy Digest

A monthly plain-English breakdown of environmental legislation moving through the region, for people with no time to read a bill.

04

Open Classroom

Free curriculum and lesson plans released for any teacher, anywhere, to run their own version of Field Literacy.
